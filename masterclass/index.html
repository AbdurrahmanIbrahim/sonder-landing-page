Before you ask

Is it live or recorded? When do I get it?

Recorded. Your login arrives by email a minute after you pay, and the lessons are yours to watch as often as you like, on your phone, tablet or computer.

Do I have to show my face or use my voice?

No. The masterclass shows three ways round it: creators who film themselves with your book, AI avatars that do the talking, and videos of just your hands and the book. The one thing a TikTok Shop video does need is the book itself on screen.

What do I need?

A book that's live on Amazon, and a phone or computer. For the TikTok Shop lessons you'll also need a TikTok Shop seller account, which is free to open (the lessons show the US shop), and printed copies to ship: 25 or more author copies at home, or a printer that ships for you. The Amazon ads bonus assumes a starting ad budget of $5 to $10 a day.

I'm not on TikTok. Is that a problem?

No, everyone starts somewhere, but TikTok is where most of my method happens, so you'll need an account. The repurposing lessons show how to send the same videos to Instagram, YouTube and Facebook automatically.

I'm not good with technology. Can I still do this?

Most lessons are recorded on my screen, so you can pause and copy each click. Apps move their buttons often, so a few screens will look different from when I recorded them.

Does it work for my kind of book?

It's built on non-fiction and gift books. Mine are a pregnancy guide for first-time dads, a gut-health book and a fill-in journal for mums, and the examples come from parenting, health, faith and money books. Fiction isn't covered.

Does anything else cost money?

The lessons don't. Doing what they teach can. TikTok Shop needs printed copies, plus free samples for creators (about $8 each with postage, in my numbers). Creators earn a commission, usually 15 to 20% of each sale, and some get a monthly retainer. Some lessons use paid tools such as HeyGen, Claude, FastMoss or BookBeam, and Amazon ads need an ad budget. A few tool links in the lessons are my affiliate links.

Is this a get-rich-quick thing?

No. It's a marketing skill, and it takes work: my first 199 videos did almost nothing. I tell everyone to expect 45 to 90 days of daily posting before anything takes off, and some books never catch on. Nobody can promise you sales, and I don't.

My book isn't out yet. Should I still buy it?
