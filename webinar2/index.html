The part nobody teaches

You did everything they told you to.

  • ✓Wrote the bookDone
  • ✓Edited and proofread itDone
  • ✓Paid for a proper coverDone
  • ✓Published it on AmazonDone
  • ✓Priced it, wrote the blurb, picked the categoriesDone
  • ✗Anybody found out it existedNot done

Five of those are entirely within your control, and you did all five. The sixth is the only one that pays — and it is the one every publishing course stops just short of. Publishing is a distribution business. The writing is the entry ticket, not the job.

Who fixed it from nothing

Lexi's book made $1,700.
Then she solved the sixth box.

Lexi Kaz
Co-founder · Sonder

Seven months to write a pregnancy guide for first-time fathers. $1,700 in its first month, sixteen reviews. By every measure of craft the book was finished and good. It was also invisible.

She published nothing for a full year, waiting until it had a hundred reviews. Then a competitor's book became the best-selling title in the entire Amazon store and she went looking for why. The answer was TikTok — a channel almost no author was using, where every serious operator she met was selling supplements. She was the only person in the room selling books.

She has fewer than 1,500 followers. She has never mentioned Amazon in a single video. What she built was not an audience — it was a way to be discovered by other people's.

"I'm just a college dropout with a dream."

  • Mar 2025Sells $230,000 of books in a month through 5,200 affiliates, on under $4,000 of Amazon ads. 87% of buyers never saw an ad.
  • Jun 2026The same first book peaks at #152 of every book on Amazon US and passes 1,001 reviews, up from 450.
  • TodaySixteen videos a day across four platforms, made in Sonder. The book is four years old and still climbing.

And who proved it the expensive way

Michael sold 107,677 books
and was still invisible.

Michael Langdon
Co-founder · Sonder

Michael spent twenty years in video production, with a client list including Speedo, Ring — Amazon's doorbell company — and TikTok itself. He has appeared on the BBC and Channel 9 in Australia talking about video and self-publishing.

Then he pointed all of it at his own books: 56 titles across three imprints, 107,677 copies, over $1.2 million at retail. He bought every single customer, one click at a time, for four years. It was profitable every year. It never produced a bestseller, and most of those 56 books earned almost nothing.

He had solved the first five boxes better than almost anyone. The sixth one he was renting, by the click, at a price that went up every year.

"I didn't write a better book. I finally had a way to tell people it existed."

Said before anyone checks

Three months is a short window, and video was not the only thing that changed. The launch was seeded to an email list we already had, review requests are automated — which is where 90 reviews at 4.9 stars came from, and reviews lift organic ranking on their own — and Amazon ads ran throughout. Video is one input among several. It is the only one that was new.
